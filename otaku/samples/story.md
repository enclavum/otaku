## Story

### Story so far

The traveling cartographer Maren came down into the Meade valley to update her survey and found the river gone from its bed — dry as old bone, the ferry up on chocks. Tallis, the old ferryman and the only soul who stayed, told her the water left three nights ago and that it went uphill, and the scour marks along the empty channel bear him out. On the ferry deck he pointed to the weir at Harrow Lock as the place the river always fought, and under his finger the inked river on Maren's own map moved. She has drawn the true course in red and set out for the lock as dusk comes down.

### System

## Scenes

### 1 · The Dry Bed
- **Messages:** 1-6

Maren, a traveling cartographer, follows the post road into the Meade valley and finds the river missing from its bed: a channel of pale dry stones, fish bones weathered far older than three days, and the ferry up on chocks with its rope still strung across the air. The only person left at the crossing is Tallis, the old ferryman. He tells her the water departed three nights ago and — quietly, like a man afraid of being overheard — that it went uphill. Maren writes his words in her notebook where he can watch her do it, choosing to treat his account as testimony rather than grief.

#### Maren

**State:** Kneeling in the dry riverbed below the ferry landing, notebook open, more curious than afraid.
**Entry:** I came down the post road to bring the Meade survey up to date and found the river gone. The bed is dry as a hearthstone, the fish bones look years old instead of days, and the ferry sits on chocks with its rope still strung. The ferryman, Tallis, says the water left three nights ago — and that it went uphill. I wrote his words down exactly, because they are either an old man's grief or the truest thing anyone has told me on this survey.

#### Tallis

**State:** Standing by his beached ferry with a coil of rope, guarded but daring to hope.
**Entry:** A mapmaker came down the road — first stranger since the water left. She didn't laugh when I said the river went uphill. She wrote it down where I could see the words, and she pressed her palm to the bed the way you'd check a sick animal for fever. Maybe her map remembers what this valley is trying to forget.

### 2 · The Map That Moved
- **Messages:** 7-10

Tallis leads Maren up the empty channel, where every mark a river leaves behind — scour grooves, combed grass, a climbing waterline — runs against the slope: the water was walking uphill when it left. Back on the ferry deck, Maren unrolls her survey and asks him to point to where the river bent wrong. His finger comes down on the weir at Harrow Lock, and before both their eyes the inked river shifts a hair's width toward his hand and holds its new course. Maren marks the true course in red ink and, with dusk coming down, sets out alone along the towpath for Harrow Lock.

**Entry:** Tallis walked me up the old channel and the banks told his story for him: scour marks running against the slope, grass combed uphill, a waterline climbing from stone to stone. On the ferry deck I asked him to point to where the river bent wrong, and his finger came down on the weir at Harrow Lock — and my map moved. The inked river leaned toward his hand and kept its new course. Maps record; they do not move. Mine moved, and I am going to find out why.

#### Tallis

**State:** Alone by his beached ferry in the failing light, ashamed, listening for water that does not come.
**History:** Tallis stayed with his beached ferry after the river left the Meade valley, telling anyone who would listen that the water had gone uphill — and no one listened until the cartographer Maren came down the post road. She took his account as testimony, read the truth of it in the scoured banks, and when her map moved under his finger at the ferry deck he gave her honest directions to Harrow Lock. He could not make himself go along, and the shame of staying sits with him by the dry landing.
**Entry:** I showed her the climb-marks and she read them straight off, the way I'd read water. Then her map twitched on my ferry deck, plain as a fish on the boards, and she didn't run — she inked the new course in red and asked the way to Harrow Lock. I gave it to her true. And I did not offer to walk with her, and I am ashamed of that.
